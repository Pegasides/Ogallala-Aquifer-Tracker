## Groundwater framing

### High Plains aquifer / predevelopment reference
- USGS describes the High Plains aquifer as underlying parts of eight states: Colorado, Kansas, Nebraska, New Mexico, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming.
- USGS publications commonly use about 1950 as the predevelopment reference associated with the onset of substantial irrigation development.
- Source: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/changes-water-levels-and-storage-high-plains-aquifer-predevelopment-2009

### Long-term decline hotspot context
- USGS states that water levels have declined more than 150 feet in some areas of the Texas Panhandle and also documents declines greater than 150 feet farther north through the Oklahoma Panhandle and Kansas.
- The Version 3 red rings should therefore remain broad regional context, not city-specific measurements.
- Source: https://www.usgs.gov/media/videos/water-level-change-high-plains-aquifer-system

### 1980 historical context
- USGS reports that by 1980, water levels in parts of Texas, Oklahoma, and southwestern Kansas had declined more than 100 feet.
- This supports showing early regional decline by 1980, but it does not independently validate the Tracker's 85 working index; that index remains an editorial comparison scale.
- Source: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/water-level-changes-high-plains-aquifer-1980-1995

## Editorial guardrails for Version 3

1. The Tracker working indices (100 / 85 / 60 / 40 and intermediate projection steps) are comparison indices, not percentages of aquifer water remaining and not USGS measurements.
2. Community markers are reference locations unless a locality-specific measurement is explicitly sourced.
3. The >150-foot decline rings are regional USGS context and must not be presented as measurements at the named community markers.
4. Data-center presence is infrastructure context, not proof of local well decline or aquifer impact.
5. When a company makes a cooling-water or operational-water claim, attribute the claim to the company.
6. Present-day data-center and hotspot layers should remain confined to the 2026 view unless a future scenario is separately sourced and clearly labeled.
7. The Groundwater Window cross-section is educational and illustrative, not a surveyed engineering profile or a literal uniform statewide water-table surface.
