Your Yard, By the Numbers

What replacing part of a lawn can mean in Alliance

≈ 623 gallons 1 inch of water applied across 1,000 sq. ft. of lawn
≈ $1.30–$2.25 Alliance urban water-use charge for that 623 gallons, depending on billing tier
1–1½ in./week UNL summer lawn-water benchmark; actual need varies with weather and soil

The household case is bigger than the water bill. A long-term water-wise landscape study found homes with mostly xeric landscaping averaged about 2.2 fewer hours of yard maintenance per month and $206 less per year in direct landscape-maintenance spending than homes with mostly turf. These were study-period results from Southern Nevada, not a promise of Alliance savings.

Save water Well-chosen native landscapes can reduce the need for supplemental irrigation once established.
Save money + time Less lawn can mean less mowing, irrigation, fertilizer and other routine maintenance.
Reduce chemical inputs Using less fertilizer and fewer pesticides reduces the amount available for runoff or movement through soil.

Protect the water beneath us. UNL reports that sustainable landscapes can reduce runoff and the movement of pollutants such as fertilizer and pesticides, while healthy roots and soil can help filter some pollutants and increase water infiltration.

Keep this in perspective: A single residential lawn is not the principal cause of Nebraska's groundwater contamination problems. The point is simpler: homeowners can reduce the water, fertilizer and pesticide inputs they personally control — and many households doing so can create a meaningful cumulative benefit.

Establishment matters: Newly planted native landscapes still require water, weed control and attention while becoming established. The larger water and maintenance advantages come after establishment.

Research & local data: University of Nebraska–Lincoln — Lawn Irrigation • University of Nebraska–Lincoln — Sustainable Landscapes • City of Alliance — Residential Water Rates • SNWA / U.S. Bureau of Reclamation — Xeriscape Conversion Study
