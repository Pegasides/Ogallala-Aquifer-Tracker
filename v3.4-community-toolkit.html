Community Infrastructure Protection Framework

A Single-Sheet Model Demand Document & Ordinance Template for Local Municipalities

Part 1: Public Intervention & Demand Layout

To the City Council / County Commission:

Infrastructure expansion must not compromise local resources. We demand an immediate administrative pause on all data center zoning, grid interconnections, and building permits until the following non-negotiable infrastructure guardrails are legally codified:

Resource CoreCommunity Threshold / Enforceable Guardrail
Grid & Ratepayer Protection100% Capital Responsibility: The developer must legally fund all electrical substations, grid upgrades, and high-voltage transmission lines. Utility rate structures must isolate residential consumers from system-wide capacity price spikes.
Water ConservationClosed-Loop Mandate: No surface-water or evaporative cooling systems allowed. Facilities must utilize 100% air-cooled or closed-loop cooling infrastructure utilizing less than 5,000 gallons per day baseline consumption.
Acoustic SetbacksDecibel Limits & Buffers: Continuous industrial cooling fan noise shall not exceed 45 dBA at the property line. Industrial zoning must enforce a mandatory 1,500-foot buffer from residential property lines.

Part 2: Model Zoning & Utility Moratorium Ordinance Language

Municipalities may use the following model language as a starting point to enact an immediate freeze, establish regulatory jurisdiction, and revise comprehensive development codes.

Section 1: Title & Declaration of Moratorium

The City/County hereby imposes a comprehensive administrative moratorium on the acceptance, review, processing, and approval of any zoning amendments, special use permits, site plans, or building permits for high-density, large-scale computing facilities, commonly referred to as “Data Centers,” utilizing a combined concurrent electrical demand exceeding ten (10) Megawatts (MW) or an aggregate water consumption exceeding five thousand (5,000) gallons per day.

Section 2: Scope of Resource & Utility Auditing

During the active period of this Moratorium, no municipal utility provider or water authority under the jurisdiction of this Board shall execute a service capability agreement or allocate capacity to a Large-Scale Computing Facility until a comprehensive Cumulative Resource Impact Assessment is completed. Said Assessment must independently verify:

(a) that local water tables or aquifer drawdowns will undergo 0.0% net-negative impact;
(b) that local grid stability is maintained with zero risk of brownouts; and
(c) that infrastructure upgrade costs are borne exclusively by the applicant.

Section 3: Duration & Extension Protocol

This Moratorium shall take effect immediately upon adoption and shall remain in full force and effect for a period of three hundred sixty-five (365) days, or until the municipal zoning commission completes a modernized, resource-isolated Comprehensive Zoning and Utility Code Overlay. The Board reserves the right to extend this framework for an additional one hundred eighty (180) days via simple majority vote if the impact studies require further scientific verification.

Important: This is an educational model and not legal advice. Municipal authority, utility regulation, moratorium procedures, evidentiary standards and enforceability vary by jurisdiction. A city or county attorney and qualified technical experts should review and adapt all language before introduction or adoption.

Organizer’s Guide to the Framework

How the printable framework is organized for local organizers, city councils and county commissions

1. The Community Demand Document Layout

  • Grid & Ratepayer Firewalls: Establishes a 100% developer-funded upgrade requirement, legally shielding local residential ratepayers from capacity-driven price spikes.
  • Water Conservation Guardrails: Mandates an explicit transition away from evaporative cooling to zero-surface-water, air-cooled, or closed-loop systems, capping total daily consumption at 5,000 gallons.
  • Acoustic Setbacks: Imposes a strict 45 dBA continuous noise limit at the property line and enforces a mandatory 1,500-foot buffer from residential zoning borders.

2. Model Zoning & Utility Moratorium Ordinance Language

  • Section 1 — Administrative Freeze: Standardized legislative text that institutes a 365-day hold on accepting, processing, or approving permits or special-use exemptions for computing facilities exceeding 10 Megawatts (MW).
  • Section 2 — Resource Isolation & Capacity Auditing: Language blocking municipal utility networks or water authorities from allocating utility headroom until an independent, developer-funded Cumulative Resource Impact Assessment addresses local aquifer drawdowns, grid stability and infrastructure costs.
  • Section 3 — Duration and Overlay Development: A protocol explaining how the moratorium remains active while the zoning board constructs a resource-isolated comprehensive zoning overlay, with a possible 180-day extension when further scientific verification is required.

Suggested Use

Organizers may print this overview as a short introduction and attach the full framework behind it. Local officials can use the documents to identify policy questions, request technical evidence and begin jurisdiction-specific drafting with municipal counsel.

Important: This guide summarizes a model advocacy framework. It does not guarantee that any threshold, moratorium or ordinance provision is lawful, technically feasible or enforceable in a particular community. Obtain local legal and technical review.
