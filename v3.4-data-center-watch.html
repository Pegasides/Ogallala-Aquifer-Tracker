Reuters · September 14, 2026

Texas Orders Penalties for Data Centers That Withhold Water Data

Gov. Greg Abbott directed the Texas Water Development Board to compel major users, including data centers, to file required water-use reports and impose legal consequences for past and future noncompliance. Under the cited Water Code provisions, nonrespondents can be referred for misdemeanor prosecution and become ineligible for certain state water permits; the board must report initial enforcement progress by October 14. The directive and statutory remedies are verified government actions, while Abbott’s statement that facilities appear to have committed violations is an allegation—no named data center has been adjudicated in the reporting.

Axios Kansas City · September 15, 2026

Kansas Data-Center Fight Moves Into Governor’s Race After Local Recall

Data centers became a Kansas gubernatorial issue after Democratic candidate Cindy Holscher endorsed a pause and Republican candidate Ty Masterson opposed a statewide moratorium, although both voted for the state’s 2025 incentive law. The debate follows Independence voters’ 68%–32% recall of a council member who supported a Nebius project. The incentive vote and recall result are documented records; the candidates’ resource and economic assurances and an advocacy coalition’s moratorium demand are political positions, not findings about future project impacts.

Reuters · September 18, 2026

Virginia Moves to End Data-Center Secrecy and Promote Cleaner Backup Power

Virginia unveiled a Data Center Accountability Framework that would bar nondisclosure agreements for projects of at least 25 megawatts and offer incentives for solar- and wind-based backup generation instead of natural gas or diesel. The framework is intended to address secrecy, electricity bills and environmental effects in the world’s largest data-center market. Its release is a verified government action, but Reuters reports that some provisions require General Assembly approval next year, so not all restrictions are binding; promised rate, transparency and environmental benefits remain policy goals rather than measured outcomes.

Reuters · September 16, 2026

House Passes First Federal Data-Center Ratepayer Bill, 417–3

The U.S. House approved H.R. 9340, the Ratepayer Protection Act, requiring state utility commissions to consider a standard under which data centers drawing more than 100 megawatts pay the full incremental cost of power and transmission infrastructure built to serve them. The recorded vote and bill provisions are verified congressional actions, but the measure remains a federal recommendation that preserves state authority; it has not passed the Senate or become law. Supporters call it a first consumer safeguard, while critics argue that merely requiring states to consider the standard is too weak.

Colorado Public Radio / KRCC · September 17, 2026

Colorado Springs Delays Project Taurus Until After Municipal Election

The Colorado Springs City Council voted 7–2 to postpone its decision on Raeden’s proposed 50-megawatt Project Taurus until April 13, 2027, one week after the municipal election. Council members said the delay allows time to examine a large-load utility contract and develop data-center standards after extensive public testimony focused on power, water, low-frequency noise and neighborhood effects. The postponement is a verified municipal action, not a project rejection; promised tax and business benefits, the developer’s mitigation assurances and residents’ health or environmental warnings remain stakeholder claims.
