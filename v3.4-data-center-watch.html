Reuters · September 9, 2026

U.S. Power Use Expected to Reach New Records as AI Demand Rises

The U.S. Energy Information Administration projects national electricity use will increase from 4,195 billion kilowatt-hours in 2025 to 4,270 billion in 2026 and 4,349 billion in 2027. Reuters reports that AI-intensive data centers, cryptocurrency operations and broader electrification are among the demand drivers. These figures are agency forecasts, not measured future consumption.

Reuters · September 10, 2026

House Schedules Vote on Data-Center Ratepayer Protection Bill

A spokesperson for House Speaker Mike Johnson confirmed that the bipartisan Ratepayer Protection Act will reach the House floor next week. The measure seeks to prevent electricity-system costs tied to data-center expansion from being shifted to ordinary households. The scheduled vote is a verified procedural development, but the House has not yet voted and the promised customer protection remains a legislative objective—not an established outcome.

Associated Press · September 11, 2026

Federal Court Rejects Emergency Order Keeping Coal Plant Open

A unanimous federal appeals-court panel ruled that the Energy Department exceeded its authority when it forced Michigan’s J.H. Campbell coal plant to remain open past its planned retirement. The administration had cited rising demand from artificial intelligence and data centers, while financial filings put continued-operation losses at about $259 million that opponents say could reach Midwest customers. The ruling is a verified judicial action; claims about avoided blackouts, health effects and who ultimately pays remain agency or stakeholder positions.

City of St. Louis · September 11, 2026

St. Louis Sends Comprehensive Data-Center Zoning Rules to Mayor

The Board of Aldermen passed Board Bill 49 by a 13–0 final vote and delivered it to the mayor, creating comprehensive zoning regulations for future data centers. Reported provisions address siting, noise, heat, closed-loop water conservation and renewable electricity, while the previously approved 120-megawatt Armory project would proceed under separate conditions. The vote and delivery are verified government actions, but the bill has not yet been signed and projected environmental or economic effects remain unproven.

Axios · September 10, 2026

EPA Moves Could Ease Air-Permit Rules for Data Centers

EPA is considering—but has not formally proposed—treating some temporary gas turbines as mobile equipment rather than stationary pollution sources. The agency has separately proposed reducing federal public-participation requirements for smaller-source permits and allowing more construction before permits, and its guidance generally excludes off-grid plants from the Acid Rain Program. Those proposals and guidance are verified agency actions; EPA says they streamline development without weakening protections, while legal and environmental groups’ warnings about pollution, permit splitting and reduced oversight are critiques or allegations.
