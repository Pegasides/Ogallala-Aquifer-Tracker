Evaporative cooling uses continuous water consumption through evaporation, whereas closed-loop systems recycle the same water indefinitely within a sealed pipeline. This shift fundamentally changes how data centers impact local water supplies, utility costs, and resource efficiency.

Here is a direct comparison of how these two cooling methodologies function and what the transition means for data center construction and community resources.

Technical Breakdown: Evaporative vs. Closed-Loop

Feature Old Evaporative Cooling New Closed-Loop Cooling
Water Consumption High. Water is continuously added and evaporated to reject heat into the atmosphere. Near Zero. A fixed volume of water is sealed inside the pipes and constantly recycled.
Local Infrastructure Impact Severe. Demands massive daily volume from municipal supplies or local aquifers. Minimal. Requires a significant one-time fill, but places negligible ongoing demand on local water infrastructure.
Water Treatment Needs Complex. Constant evaporation leaves behind concentrated minerals (scaling), requiring chemical treatment and wastewater disposal. Low. Because the system is sealed, the water remains uncontaminated, eliminating continuous chemical treatments.
Energy Consumption Highly Efficient. Uses less electricity because it leverages the natural cooling power of evaporating water. Variable. Can require more electricity to run compressors or large air-cooled fans, though modern "free-cooling" designs minimize this spike.

The Infrastructure Pivot: Why Data Centers are Abandoning the Water Grid

The era of the multi-million-gallon-a-day data center is coming to a close. As local communities protect vulnerable aquifers and zoning boards tighten resource extraction rules, hyperscale construction is undergoing its most massive engineering shift in a generation.

This infographic breaks down the structural and environmental differences between legacy cooling systems and modern sustainable engineering:

  • Traditional Evaporative Facilities rely on continuous water extraction. They pour millions of gallons into open cooling towers, allowing natural evaporation to reject server heat. This demands sprawling, industrial-scale infrastructure—including massive exposed stabilization reservoirs—pushing the total campus footprint to 5.2 million square feet. The environmental cost is high: excessive local water strain and thousands of gallons of chemically treated wastewater dumped daily.
  • Closed-Loop Chiller Architecture solves the water problem by shifting entirely to an internal, recycled lifecycle. Liquid is permanently sealed inside a heavy-duty piping network and cooled by massive rooftop heat exchanger arrays and computerized fans. Because water is never exposed to the air, consumption drops to near zero. This compact, self-contained design drastically streamlines land use, compressing the necessary campus footprint down to 3.5 million square feet.

While closed-loop systems require significantly less land and protect local water tables, they demand more electricity to run the massive mechanical cooling arrays. It is a direct trade-off: saving local water by increasing power efficiency demands.

Operational Expenditure (OpEx) — Ongoing Running Costs

Closed-loop facilities dramatically reduce mechanical maintenance costs, but trade them for higher electricity bills.

  • The Power Penalty (Higher OpEx): Open towers leverage the natural laws of physics to cool via evaporation, using very little electricity. Closed-loop systems must physically force air across hot coils using massive motorized fan walls. This increases the facility’s Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) metric, directly raising monthly utility power bills.
  • Near-Zero Water & Chemical Costs (Lower OpEx): Legacy towers must continuously buy municipal water to replace what evaporates. They also spend heavily on anti-scaling chemicals and wastewater disposal fees. Closed-loop systems require a one-time water purchase at startup and virtually zero ongoing water or chemical treatment expenses.
  • Streamlined Preventive Maintenance (Lower OpEx): Traditional towers struggle constantly with mineral scale buildup, biological growth, and algae, requiring frequent manual cleanings and shutdowns. Closed-loop plumbing remains perfectly clean internally, drastically reducing labor hours and equipment replacement cycles.
